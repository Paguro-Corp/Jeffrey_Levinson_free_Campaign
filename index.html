What the grid shows now. Product shots, a classic roadster, a retail appearance at Harrods, a performance reel and a few portraits with the clutch. Each post stands alone, so the grid reads as a collection of moments rather than one world.

What it does not show yet. The clutch in use at the events it is made for, the same woman returning week after week, and a clear next step for the follower who wants one. Audit of the public profile, October 2026.

Three campaigns

One woman, three evenings.

Each campaign runs for one week: a static image on Monday, a carousel on Wednesday and a short reel on Saturday. Each one belongs to one clutch, one event and one look, so every week feels new while the woman stays the same.

Instagram

  • MondayStatic post in the feed, 4:5, the image that stops the scroll.
  • WednesdayCarousel of four slides. Built to be saved and shared.
  • SaturdayReel, 9:16, made from the same scenes, to reach people who do not follow the brand yet.
  • Every dayA comment with the keyword opens a private DM through ManyChat, with the piece, the finish and where to buy.

Facebook

  • Same dayThe same static, carousel and Reel, published from Meta Business Suite at the same time as Instagram.
  • AudienceReaches the clients who spend more time on Facebook than on Instagram, often an older, established audience.
  • Every dayThe same keyword works in Facebook comments and answers in Messenger through ManyChat.
  • Ads readyEvery piece is cut in 4:5 and 9:16, so the best performer can be boosted on both platforms without new work.

Why it works

  • Mirror chrome reflects the room around it, so every scene becomes part of the product.
  • The design of the clutch is engineered, and the setting says so without a single spec line.

Why move beyond the current feed

From showing the clutch to living with it.

The current content proves the product is beautiful. These campaigns keep that standard and add what makes a follower stay, save and write: a person, a place to go and a reason to answer.
